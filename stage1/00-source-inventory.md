> **STANDING REQUIREMENT — most important architectural requirement (user, 2026-11-12).**
> The application must still produce reliable actuarial calculations and documented acceptance decisions when exact historical reconstruction is impossible. That is what distinguishes this system from an ordinary pension benefit calculator. Design for it from the start: bounds, scenarios, documented dispositions (Verified / Supported for acceptance / Requires investigation / Correction recommended / Indeterminate), and a human decision — never a silent failure or a made-up exact number when the evidence cannot support one.
>
> **Implication of this inventory for that requirement:** this inventory establishes that exact reconstruction will *frequently* be impossible from the sources that exist. No source states the annuity form, vesting treatment, or reporting convention of PA_AMB; no source states any acceptance criterion or reconciliation tolerance for the PA_AMB accept/reject decision itself; the only PBGC acceptance instrument found (the AMB Acceptance Template) is a blank memo shell with a `<body>` placeholder and no criteria; and two of the named BCV manuals could not be text-read at all (see §5). Those are not minor gaps — they are the reason the system must be built around bounds, scenarios, and documented dispositions rather than around a single reconstructed number. Every gap in §5 is tagged below with its design consequence under this requirement.

### 1.2 What the definition establishes

- **Units:** monthly benefit amount. The field name and description say "Monthly Benefit"; the data type is NUMBER. No currency is stated (USD is implied by context, not stated — do not assert it as a dictionary fact).
- **Whose number:** the *plan administrator's* number, not PBGC's. It is an **Input** field: PBGC receives it, PBGC does not calculate it.
- **Measurement date anchor:** **at DOTE** — the participant's Date of Termination of Employment (BCV1.DOTE, see §1.4). Not "at DOPT" and not "at NRD". The `DOPT` category tag on the field means only that the field is in the DOPT category group used by the dictionary (see the `background` tab: "DOPT: Fields used for values that are as of DOPT"); it does **not** override the description's explicit "at DOTE".
- **Acceptance flag exists:** BCV3.PA_AMB_ACCEPT — "Indicator for identifying when plan administrator's AMB is being accepted (Y/N)", VARCHAR2(1), category `Plan Admin / New (Release 6.3)`. This is the only dictionary field that records the accept decision itself. It carries no criterion, no tolerance, and no rationale field.

### 1.3 What the dictionary does NOT say (gaps — each is a Stage 2 design input)

The dictionary entry does **not** state any of the following. None of it may be assumed:

1. **Annuity form.** It does not say whether PA_AMB is in the plan's normal (single) form, the automatic (married) form, or any other form. Contrast BCV1.VB, which explicitly says "in the Normal Single Form (NSF) at NRA", and BCV1.VBNF, which explicitly says "in the Automatic Form". PA_AMB has no such qualifier.
2. **Vesting treatment.** It does not say whether the amount is before or after applying the vesting percentage, or whether it is the vested benefit only. Contrast BCV1.VP ("Participant's Vesting Percent at DOTE") and BCV1.VB/VBNF ("Vested Monthly Benefit …"). The Answer Book (Ch. 9, Q 9:7) says an accrued benefit is the amount "before the application of a vesting schedule" — but that is a textbook definition of *accrued benefit* generally (hierarchy level 5), not a statement about the PA_AMB field, and it must not be read into the dictionary entry.
3. **Retirement-age basis / NRD.** It does not say the benefit is expressed at normal retirement age, at NRD, or at any other age/date. This is the recurring measurement-date problem: whether the benefit is evaluated as of NRD or something else is **unresolved by the dictionary**. The only anchor given is DOTE.
4. **Rounding / precision.** No rounding rule, no decimal precision, no cents rule.
5. **What "accrued" meant to the administrator.** No formula, no accrual method (fractional vs unit credit), no service/compensation basis. The companion Input field BCV9.PA_ACOMP ("Plan Administrator Average Compensation at DOTE") is the only adjacent clue to the administrator's inputs.
6. **Blank vs zero.** The dictionary does not state what a blank PA_AMB means (missing data vs zero benefit). Per the user's standing rule: a blank is missing data, never zero unless a rule says so — and no rule found here says so.
7. **Population scope.** It does not say for which RETSTAT values PA_AMB is expected to be populated (e.g. actives vs retirees in pay, nonvested participants).

### 1.5 DOBF name-collision note

- Within the dictionary there is **no collision**: `DOBF` occurs exactly once, as BCV1.DOBF, "Participant's Date of Benefit Freeze", a participant-level DATE Input field (row 32).
- The collision risk is **across grains, not within the dictionary**: the R5 Plan Summary shell header has a plan-level `DOBF` cell (plan's date of benefit freeze), while BCV1.DOBF is a participant-level field. Any code or prompt that passes "DOBF" without saying plan-level vs participant-level will confuse the two. The PA_AMB system must always qualify DOBF by grain. (R5 shell reference: `~/workspace/pbgc_review/r5/Plan_Summary_Shell.docx`; R5 inventory not re-verified in this stage.)
- Related: the dictionary's `background` tab defines the category labels DOPT / NRD / BPD / XRD / EURD / ERD / DOR / RBD, but it does **not** define DOTE as a category label. DOTE's meaning comes only from the BCV1.DOTE description quoted above.

### 1.6 What the Answer Book adds to "accrued benefit" (hierarchy level 5 — does not override the dictionary)

- Ch. 9, Q 9:7 (2024 Edition, last updated 12/2023): "An accrued benefit is the portion of the total retirement benefit earned by the plan participant as of any given date. In a defined benefit plan, the Internal Revenue Code defines an employee's accrued benefit as the amount determined under the terms of the plan and is expressed in the form of an annual benefit commencing at normal retirement age. [I.R.C. § 411(a)(7)(A)(i)] Most plans show the employee's accrued benefit as an amount that is payable monthly rather than annually. It is also payable in the normal form as defined in the plan document. That amount is the benefit the participant would be entitled to receive (before the application of a vesting schedule) if he or she were to terminate employment or retire. There are two methods a plan can use to determine the accrued benefit: the fractional accrual method (see Q 9:8) or the unit credit accrual method (see Q 9:10)."
- Ch. 9, Q 9:8: "Under the fractional accrual method, the projected retirement benefit is multiplied by a fraction, the numerator of which is benefit service as of the date the accrued benefit is calculated, and the denominator of which is the total benefit service the participant will have at retirement." Cites Treas. Reg. § 1.411(b)-1(b)(3) for the fractional rule benefit.
- **Caution:** these are general textbook statements about accrued benefits. They do **not** state what any particular plan administrator put into PA_AMB. The system must treat the administrator's basis as an unknown to be established per case (from plan documents and the administrator's own records), not as settled by Ch. 9.

## 2. Tolerance rules — as found (the second finding this stage had to establish)

### 2.1 What WAS found: PBGC tolerance rules exist, but they govern correction of PBGC benefit determinations and payments — not the PA_AMB accept/reject decision

There **are** documented PBGC "tolerance rules". They live in **PBGC Operating Policy 5.8-1, Benefit Corrections**, and they are applied by reference in **PBGC Operating Policy 6.3-1, Underpayment Reimbursement and Interest Payments**. Their exact content:

**Policy 5.8-1, Benefit Corrections (Issue Date 10/02/2014; copy in `current-policies.pdf`, and standalone `Benefit Corrections.pdf`).** Quoted from the policy text (section D; emphasis added):

- General principle (section D, intro): "PBGC must be reasonably certain that a benefit or payment error has occurred before a benefit or payment is changed." And: "Where circumstances prevent PBGC from attaining a reasonable degree of certainty in establishing whether an error has occurred or the amount of the error, PBGC will not change the benefit or payment."
- Errors "must be corrected, except: For certain de minimis errors; In situations where a cost-benefit analysis establishes that the cost of correcting the error exceeds the anticipated savings to PBGC of correcting the error; or Where the error was previously identified and the error was not corrected under the policy in effect at that time."
- **D.1 — Corrections at benefit determination issuance (changing estimated benefits to final benefits):**
  - "Increases. An increase in the monthly amount of the benefit due or paid the payee is made regardless of the amount."
  - "Decreases. A decrease in the monthly benefit due or paid the payee is made if the decrease is **$1.00 or more**." (Reduced from $5.00 in this edition.)
- **D.3.a — Corrections after a benefit determination becomes effective, benefit in pay status:**
  - "Increase. An increase will be made only if the increase is **$1.00 or more** in the monthly benefit amount paid or due the participant or beneficiary."
  - "Decrease. A decrease will be made only if the decrease is **$5.00 or more** in the monthly benefit amount paid or due the payee."
- **D.3.b — Benefit not in pay status:** PBGC applies the D.3.a rules; "even though a benefit error may not require correction under section D.3.a., it may be more practical in some cases for PBGC to correct the error when the benefit is put into pay."
- A benefit determination "becomes effective after the 45-day appeals period established under 29 CFR § 4003.52 has ended and an appeal has not been filed", or after an Appeals Board decision.
- Lump-sum errors: "An increase will be made only if the increase in the lump-sum benefit or lump-sum payment is $1.00 or more" (D.4.a, partial — see source #8; the decrease limb was not fully extracted and must be re-read before being quoted).
- The policy was tightened under the Improper Payments Elimination and Recovery Act of 2010 (IPERA).

**Policy 6.3-1, Underpayment Reimbursement and Interest Payments (effective for reimbursement payments processed on or after 10/01/2017; in `current-policies.pdf`).** Quoted:

- "PBGC applies the 'tolerance rules' of Policy 5.8-1 Benefit Corrections to determination of pre-termination liabilities" (section A, clarifications).
- Section E.1: "PBGC will determine pre-termination liabilities (amounts owed to payees prior to DOPT, including 'residual' amounts determined owed after a prior pre-termination liability payment was made) by including PBGC interest before and after DOPT, and by applying the tolerance rules in 5.8-1 Benefit Corrections. PBGC will pay these pre-termination liabilities if a plan has sufficient assets to pay the amounts."
- General rule (section C): "PBGC pays net underpayments including backpayments, with interest, if the net underpayment plus interest totals $1 or more as described in Section 4022.81(c) of PBGC Regulations."

**Therefore:** the tolerances are **fixed dollar amounts ($1.00 / $5.00 per month)**, they are **asymmetric** (increases vs decreases, before vs after a determination becomes effective), and they are **documented PBGC policy, not a universal percentage**. They are *not* configurable by the case actuary, and they are *not* stated as a percentage of the benefit.

### 2.2 What was NOT found — the negative finding (recorded explicitly, as instructed)

**No source consulted states any tolerance rule for reconciling a prior administrator's PA_AMB against a PBGC reconstruction, or any criterion for accepting, rejecting, or modifying PA_AMB.** Specifically searched and not found:

- No dollar or percentage tolerance for |PA_AMB − reconstructed AMB| anywhere in the Book of Job materials consulted (dictionary, CASE_PROCESSING.txt, current-policies.pdf, Benefit Corrections, processing-manual.pdf [unreadable — see §5], BCV manuals, validation predicates, Answer Book chapters, AMB Acceptance Template).
- No acceptance criterion (e.g. "accept if within X") in the **AMB Acceptance Template** (`AMB Acceptance Template.docx`): it is a blank memo shell (TO / FROM / DATE / RE: Acceptance of Accrued Monthly Benefits / EIN/PN / Case Number / DOPT / DOTR / Plan Name / `<body>` placeholder / signature line). It contains no criteria, no tolerance, no checklist, no threshold.
- No PA_AMB validation predicate in `BCV-DataValidation-predicates.xlsx` (249 data rows, IDs 200+; zero mention PA_AMB, AMB, tolerance, or administrator; see source #9).
- No mention of PA_AMB at all in CASE_PROCESSING.txt (0 hits), in the BCV add-ins documentation (0 hits for `PA_`), or in the BCV manual combined PDF (text unreadable — see §5).
- The word "tolerance" occurs in exactly two of the extracted texts searched: `Benefit_Corrections.txt` and `CASE_PROCESSING.txt` (the latter only in the phrase "risk tolerance" in an internal-controls passage, line 1194 — unrelated to benefit reconciliation). It occurs nowhere in the Answer Book chapter texts, the BCV add-ins text, or the ERISAfication text.

### 2.3 Consequence for the design (standing requirement)

- The MASTER_PROMPT §10 direction — "Reconciliation must use configurable, documented tolerances. Do not impose an arbitrary universal dollar or percentage tolerance." — is a **design decision imposed by the prompt**, not a PBGC rule found in any source. The system must therefore treat the reconciliation tolerance as a **case-level, documented, human-set parameter** (with a default that is explicitly labeled as a default, not as a PBGC rule), and record the tolerance actually applied in the audit trail for each determination.
- The 5.8-1 tolerances ($1.00 / $5.00) must **not** be silently repurposed as the PA_AMB reconciliation tolerance. They govern a different decision (correcting a PBGC benefit determination/payment after it exists). If Stage 2 ever cites them, it must cite them only as the nearest PBGC analogue, with the scope difference stated.
- The "reasonably certain" standard in 5.8-1 is, however, directly usable as a design principle: a disposition of Verified / Correction recommended requires reasonable certainty about both the error and its amount; where that certainty cannot be attained, the disposition must be Requires investigation or Indeterminate — never a forced exact number.

## 4. Conflicts and tensions between sources (flagged, not silently resolved)

1. **Tension — what "accrued benefit" means vs what PA_AMB contains (not a direct conflict).** Ch. 9, Q 9:7 (level 5) defines accrued benefit as the amount "before the application of a vesting schedule", payable monthly in the plan's normal form at normal retirement age. The dictionary entry for PA_AMB (level 2 system definition, authoritative for the field) says none of those things — no form, no vesting, no NRA basis. **Resolution for Stage 2:** do not import the Ch. 9 qualifiers into PA_AMB. Treat form / vesting / age basis as per-case unknowns to be established from plan documents and the administrator's records, and carry them as explicit basis fields on every comparison (MASTER_PROMPT §3 typed quantity).
2. **Tension — measurement date: DOTE vs NRD vs DOPT.** The dictionary anchors PA_AMB "at DOTE" (termination of employment). The user's recurring case question is whether the benefit is evaluated as of NRD or something else. Ch. 9 expresses accrued benefits at normal retirement age; BCV1.AMB is also "at DOTE" but is a Calculated field on PBGC's basis. **Resolution for Stage 2:** the system must record the measurement date as a first-class, possibly-unresolved field on every BenefitObservation; a PA_AMB with no established measurement date is an unresolved comparison basis (MASTER_PROMPT §5), not a number to compare anyway.
3. **Apparent conflict — checked and NOT a conflict: DOBF.** R5 header DOBF (plan-level) vs BCV1.DOBF (participant-level Date of Benefit Freeze) differ in grain, not in meaning. No action beyond qualifying the grain (§1.5).
4. **No conflict found** between the dictionary and the tolerance policies: they govern different things (field definition vs correction of PBGC determinations). The risk is misapplication, not contradiction — see §2.3.

## 6. Implications for Stage 2 (what this inventory forces the design to handle)

These follow directly from the sources above and the standing requirement. They are inventory consequences, not new design decisions:

1. **PA_AMB basis is unknown until proven per case.** Form, vesting, age basis, rounding, and measurement date are not in the dictionary. The canonical input schema (Stage 1 deliverable 5) must carry a *basis record* with every PA_AMB value, with an explicit "unresolved" state that blocks a like-for-like comparison but does not block the case (it routes to Requires investigation / Indeterminate, with bounds where possible).
2. **The comparison pair is PA_AMB (Input, BCV9) vs AMB (Calculated, BCV1), both "at DOTE"** — but only after the bases are established as equivalent (MASTER_PROMPT §3). A numeric difference with mismatched bases is not a discrepancy; it is an unresolved basis.
3. **Blanks are missing data.** No source equates a blank PA_AMB with zero. The validation spec must treat blank as missing, with a missing-data pattern analysis (MASTER_PROMPT §12), never a silent zero-fill.
4. **No PBGC reconciliation tolerance exists to encode.** The tolerance is a documented, human-set, case-level parameter. The 5.8-1 $1.00/$5.00 tolerances govern a different decision and must not be repurposed silently. The audit log must record the tolerance applied to each determination.
5. **The acceptance memo has no criteria to automate.** The AMB Acceptance Template is a shell. The system's output must therefore be the *evidence package* for the memo (recorded benefit, reconstruction/bounds, basis, differences, hypotheses, missing evidence, recommended disposition, human determination) — not an auto-filled verdict.
6. **Two named manuals are unread.** Nothing in Stage 2 may claim support from `processing-manual.pdf` or `bcv-manual-combined.pdf` until their text is recovered (OCR / re-encoded copy).
7. **ERISAfication is a discrepancy hypothesis, not an error.** A PA_AMB computed under the plan as written can legitimately differ from an ERISAfied reconstruction. The diagnostic engine must include "plan provisions as read in under ERISAfication" as an explicit hypothesis.
8. **Q 16:7 defines the minimum handoff.** When administration changes hands, the new administrator needs salary history, accrued benefits, and past distributions per participant. A case file missing any of the three cannot support exact reconstruction — that is precisely the situation the bounds/scenarios/dispositions design exists for.
9. **The "reasonably certain" standard (5.8-1) is the disposition gate.** Verified / Correction recommended require reasonable certainty about the error *and* its amount; anything less is Requires investigation or Indeterminate. Numerical agreement alone is not proof of correctness (MASTER_PROMPT §14).
